Career Evaluation

Career Evaluation Scorecard

Purpose

The exploratory analysis showed that the Data Scientist–classified postings in Software Publishers cover several different kinds of jobs. This page compares those role segments and ranks them with a simple scorecard, to answer one question: which segment is the best bet for a student or early-career job seeker?

Scorecard Factors

Factor Measured as Weight
Pay Median salary midpoint 30%
Accessibility Share of postings asking for 3 years of experience or less 25%
Demand Number of postings 20%
Skill fit Team skill ratings, weighted by how often the segment asks for each skill 15%
Remote Share of postings that are fully remote 10%

Accessibility gets a high weight because the product is aimed at students and early-career job seekers. Remote gets the lowest because most postings don’t say whether they’re remote.

Two segments are left out of the scoring. Data Scientist / ML has only 5 postings, which is too few to compare. AI Data Trainer postings don’t list experience requirements or any of the tracked technical skills, so two of the five factors can’t be measured for them.

Benchmark Table

Code 
SKILL_COLUMNS = {
    "Python": "skill_python",
    "SQL": "skill_sql",
    "Machine Learning": "skill_machine_learning",
    "Statistics": "skill_statistics",
    "AWS": "skill_aws",
    "Azure": "skill_azure",
    "GCP": "skill_gcp",
    "Tableau": "skill_tableau",
    "Power BI": "skill_power_bi",
    "Excel": "skill_excel",
    "Spark": "skill_spark",
}

member_columns = ["Dylan Thomson", "Richard Wu", "Sae Hee Wilson"]
team_average = team_ratings.set_index("Skill")[member_columns].mean(axis=1)

EXCLUDED_SEGMENTS = ["Data Scientist / ML", "AI Data Trainer"]
scored = panel[~panel["role_segment"].isin(EXCLUDED_SEGMENTS)]

rows = []

for segment, group in scored.groupby("role_segment"):
    # Share of postings in this segment that ask for each skill
    skill_demand = pd.Series(
        {skill: group[column].mean() for skill, column in SKILL_COLUMNS.items()}
    )

    # Team proficiency (out of 5), weighted by how often each skill is requested
    skill_fit = (skill_demand * team_average / 5).sum() / skill_demand.sum()

    stated_experience = group["MIN_YEARS_EXPERIENCE"].dropna()

    rows.append({
        "Role Segment": segment,
        "Postings": len(group),
        "Median Salary ($)": group["SALARY_MIDPOINT"].median(),
        "% Entry-Level (≤3 yrs)": (stated_experience <= 3).mean() * 100,
        "% Remote": group["is_remote"].mean() * 100,
        "Team Skill Fit (%)": skill_fit * 100,
    })

benchmark = pd.DataFrame(rows).sort_values("Postings", ascending=False)

Data / BI Analyst roles are the easiest to get into. About 47% of the postings that list an experience requirement ask for 3 years or less, compared with about 10% for Consultant / Implementation and 7% for Architect / Engineer.

Architect / Engineer roles pay the most, with a median around $187,550, but they’re the hardest to get into and the worst match for our skills (about 34% fit). Most of that gap is cloud platforms, where all three of us rated ourselves as beginners.

Sales / Pre-Sales roles are the most likely to be remote (about 56%) but have the lowest pay of the scored segments.

Opportunity Score

Each factor is rescaled from 0 (worst segment) to 1 (best segment) so factors in different units can be combined, then weighted as shown above.

Code 
WEIGHTS = {
    "Median Salary ($)": 0.30,
    "% Entry-Level (≤3 yrs)": 0.25,
    "Postings": 0.20,
    "Team Skill Fit (%)": 0.15,
    "% Remote": 0.10,
}

scores = benchmark[["Role Segment"]].copy()

for column in WEIGHTS:
    values = benchmark[column]
    scores[column] = (values - values.min()) / (values.max() - values.min())

scores["Opportunity Score"] = sum(
    scores[column] * weight for column, weight in WEIGHTS.items()
) * 100

scores = scores.sort_values("Opportunity Score", ascending=False)

fig = px.bar(
    scores.sort_values("Opportunity Score"),
    x="Opportunity Score",
    y="Role Segment",
    orientation="h",
    title="Opportunity Score by Role Segment (0–100)",
    template=PLOT_TEMPLATE
)

fig.update_layout(showlegend=False, yaxis_title="")
fig.show()

Data / BI Analyst comes out on top with a score of about 61, mainly because it’s the most accessible segment and a good match for our skills. Other Business / Analyst is second at about 54. Consultant / Implementation is third at about 43; it has the most openings, but very few of them are entry-level.

Checking the Weights

The weights are a judgment call, so we reran the ranking with all five factors weighted equally to see whether the order changes.

Code 
scores["Equal-Weight Score"] = scores[list(WEIGHTS)].mean(axis=1) * 100

comparison = scores[["Role Segment", "Opportunity Score", "Equal-Weight Score"]].copy()
comparison["Rank (Our Weights)"] = comparison["Opportunity Score"].rank(ascending=False).astype(int)
comparison["Rank (Equal Weights)"] = comparison["Equal-Weight Score"].rank(ascending=False).astype(int)

comparison.round(1)

What This Means for a Job Seeker

For a student aiming at data science in software publishing, a Data / BI Analyst role is the most realistic first job. It’s the most open to early-career candidates, has the second-most openings, and pays a median of about $130,000.

Architect / Engineer roles are a longer-term goal. They pay the most but usually want around 8 years of experience plus cloud skills.

Consultant / Implementation roles are plentiful but mostly mid-career, and most are at Oracle. They suit people who already have ERP or industry experience.

Limitations

Segments are assigned from keywords in job titles, so some postings will be in the wrong group. Accessibility only counts postings that list an experience requirement. Remote status is missing for most postings, which is why it has the lowest weight. Skill fit is based on our own self-ratings, so another student’s scores would come out differently.
